# challoc architecture review, 2026-09-17

A step-back review of the whole allocator for performance, written after the
implementation loop was stopped with Phase 0 and Phase 1 implemented and
Phase 1 acceptance failing on one criterion (post-delete RSS in the Valkey
soak). Nothing in this document was implemented; every number comes from
runs made for this review on the two GCE reference VMs at commit `dacbedd`
(the iteration-12 sweep, whose working tree carries the *unmeasured*
address-ordered compacting cache flush in `src/heap.rs`), recorded in:

- `bench/results/2026-09-17-gce-review-counters.md`: perf counters for
  larson (1 and 10 threads), xmalloc-test and alloc-test under challoc,
  jemalloc 5.3.1 and mimalloc 3.5.2 on both VMs; perf record attribution;
  instruction-level annotation of the fast paths; a `narenas` A/B.
- `bench/results/2026-09-17-gce-review-soak.md`: a 600 s Valkey soak on the
  arm64 VM under challoc (compacting flush) and jemalloc, then challoc with
  the pre-compacting flush (8df225f), with the per-class slab occupancy
  table of `MEMORY MALLOC-STATS` sampled every 20 s.

Mac numbers are not used anywhere here.

## 1. Summary

The fast paths are not the problem. Single-threaded, challoc executes the
same number of instructions per larson operation as jemalloc within 4% and is
7% *faster* than jemalloc on x86_64 (68 vs 73 cycles per malloc+free pair)
and 2% slower on aarch64 (67.4 vs 65.9). The two gaps the plan is stuck on
have one structural cause each:

1. **The larson gap is cross-thread sharing of slabs, not the fast path.**
   challoc runs one arena by default and every thread refills from the same
   lowest-address partial slab, so neighbouring slots of one cache line
   belong to different threads and the descriptor bitmap words every `free`
   reads are written by every other thread's refill and flush. At 10
   threads this shows as +0.5 (x86_64) to +0.7 (aarch64) L1D misses per
   operation and +0.05 L2 misses per operation against jemalloc, all of
   which lands as backend stall: +5.6 cycles per operation on aarch64 (83.3
   vs 77.7) and +10 on x86_64 (126 vs 116). A third of larson's *own* cycles
   under challoc sit on the first-byte store into the freshly allocated
   block, a coherence miss on a line shared with another thread's slot.
   `MALLOC_CONF=narenas:16` removes it: challoc drops to 1.70 L1D misses per
   operation (jemalloc default: 2.20) and 117 cycles per operation (jemalloc
   default: 114), and jemalloc forced to one arena loses 19%. Section 3.1.
2. **The post-delete RSS residue is LIFO reuse in the thread cache, not
   slab geometry and not the flush order.** A one-class churn probe
   (`bench/probes/churn.c`) shows it directly: after a random 56% deletion,
   20 s of free-one/allocate-one churn at 19 M ops/s leaves challoc's 469
   slabs at 44.6% with a 128-slot cache, with an 8-slot cache, and with the
   compacting flush of dacbedd in the build, because the cache's length never
   moves under a balanced churn, so it never refills or flushes and every
   allocation gets the slot that was just freed. With `tcache:false` the
   same class compacts from 469 to 208 slabs in three seconds. jemalloc
   drains slowly (4.7% of its 1 KiB slabs in 20 s) only because its GC runs
   on an allocation-byte schedule and forces a few refills from the lowest
   slab per event. The 600 s Valkey soak of this review shows the same flat
   43..45% under the real workload with the compacting flush. The smallest
   change that can pass Phase 1 is therefore a cache that serves a scheduled
   fraction of allocations from its lowest-address slot and flushes its
   highest-address half on the same schedule (P1); jemalloc's GC shape is
   not enough on 64-region slabs.

Ranked proposals (details in section 4):

| # | proposal | closes | throughput effect | post-delete RSS effect | risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | scheduled address-ordered pops and flushes in the thread cache (one allocation in `2^k` takes the lowest cached slot; the highest half is flushed on the same schedule) | Phase 1 RSS | -1..-3% larson (a cold slot once in `2^k` allocations) | removes the residue rather than shrinking it; the churn probe is the unit test | low-medium; `heap.rs` cache code only, testable on the host |
| P2 | thread-affine slab ownership: refill prefers a slab this heap last claimed from, `narenas` default > 1 as the fallback | larson 10-thread gap on both VMs, xmalloc-test at 16 threads | +4.5% (aarch64) / +7..10% (x86_64) larson at 10 threads, measured with `narenas:16` | neutral to slightly worse (per-thread partial slabs) | medium; touches `plan`, stats and the defragmenter's view of bins |
| P3 | dense per-thread cache headers inside `Heap` and one contiguous slot block per heap | 1-thread residue, aarch64 dTLB | +1..3% (one dependent load fewer on `malloc`, 52 metadata pages fewer per thread) | none | medium; `heap.rs` layout, no policy change |
| P4 | descriptor and cache pages on 2 MiB pages (`metadata_thp`) or interleaved descriptor rows | aarch64 L1 dTLB refills (2x jemalloc's) | 0..2% aarch64, ~0 x86_64 | none | low (THP on metadata only) |
| P5 | the compacting flush of dacbedd: keep its form as P1's flush, expect nothing from it alone | nothing on its own (soak and probe: no drain) | neutral (measured 2026-09-16) except a 2% xmalloc-test loss on x86_64 | none alone | low |
| P6 | 4..8-region slabs for the 512..1024-byte classes | Phase 1 RSS floor if P1 is not enough | -2..-4% larson (measured for 16 KiB everywhere) | matches jemalloc's 86% occupancy in the 1024 class | medium; rejected once, only behind P1 |
| P7 | cache capacity ladder: `MIN(nregs * k, max)` like jemalloc for classes above 512 B | RSS held in caches, refill/flush lock work | neutral | small direct gain (a 128-slot cache of 1 KiB slots per thread is 128 KiB of unaccounted residue per class) | low |
| P8 | `thp:always` for slab regions as a documented opt-in | TLB for users who accept fork CoW cost | mimalloc's 0.17 dTLB refills per op vs 3.9 | RSS up | low, opt-in only |

Conflicts: P2 changes what "lowest partial slab" means and interacts with
the defragmenter (section 4, P2); P5 and P1 both decide which cached slots
go back and must be designed together; P6 costs what P2 gains and should
only be tried if P1 leaves the criterion unmet.

## 3. Reading the architecture against the numbers

### 3.1 Slabs with no owning thread (design sections 4, 5, 13.3)

The design's central choice is that slabs belong to a class, not a thread:
bitmaps are atomic, any thread refills from the class's lowest partial slab,
any thread's flush releases into any slab, and "challoc does not need arenas
for scalability". The upside is real and measured: xmalloc-test, whose every
free is remote, costs challoc 0.26 L1D misses per free against jemalloc's
1.59 and mimalloc's 3.7, because a remote free is the same code as a local
one. The downside is what the larson counters show. Under one arena, ten
threads refilling the same class take interleaved runs of slots from the
same slab (`plan` hands each refill up to `cap / 2` slots from the lowest
partial slab, so consecutive refills by different threads get adjacent
runs), and:

- Every class whose slot size is not a multiple of 64 bytes has slots that
  straddle a cache line, and the first and last lines of every refill run
  are shared with the neighbouring thread's run. larson writes the first and
  last byte of every block it allocates; a third of larson's own cycles under
  challoc sit on that store. This is user-data false sharing created by the
  allocator, and it explains why the Mac sampler kept blaming `main`.
- The descriptor bitmap word that `free` loads to validate the pointer is
  written by every refill (`fetch_or`) and flush (`fetch_and`) of every
  thread that shares the slab. 37% of `free`'s cycles wait for that load.
  The same word is what makes the `free` check exact, so the check itself is
  not the cost; its sharing is. (The 2026-09-16 probe that dropped the check
  measured 7% on the Mac; the counters say most of that was sharing, since
  at one thread the descriptor load costs nothing visible.)
- `plan` and `release` take the class lock once per refill and per flush;
  with 1024-slot caches that is rare and the lock does not appear in any
  profile. Contention is not the problem; coherence is.

jemalloc avoids all of this by default with one arena per thread (its
automatic `narenas` is four times the CPU count), and pays for it in
xmalloc-test. mimalloc avoids it with thread-owned pages and pays more.
challoc with `narenas:16` gets jemalloc's larson number while keeping its
xmalloc-test number (remote frees still land in the freeing thread's cache
keyed by the descriptor's arena, one extra load), which says the fix does
not have to give up the remote-free design. See P2 for the two ways to get
there and what each costs the defragmenter.

### 3.2 Cache shape, GC and the post-delete residue (design 6.2, 6.4)

Three things differ from jemalloc's tcache, and their combination is the
RSS failure:

1. **Capacity.** challoc's default ladder is 1024 slots for classes up to
   128 bytes, halving per doubling: 128 slots at 640..1024 bytes. jemalloc's
   is `min(2 * nregs, 200)`, floor 20: with its 4-, 16- and 32-region slabs
   in the 512..1024-byte bins that is 20..64 slots. Valkey 9.1.2 does not set
   `je_malloc_conf` (checked in the tag's sources), so these are the caps in
   play in the soak. A 128-slot cache of 1 KiB slots per thread is 128 KiB
   of `allocated`-but-idle memory per class per thread and, more
   importantly, 128 slots that never reach a slab while the cache is neither
   empty nor full.
2. **GC cadence.** jemalloc runs `tcache_gc` from the *fast path's* byte
   counter: every 64 KiB allocated (and every 20 KiB deallocated) one bin,
   round robin, has three quarters of its low-water mark flushed back to the
   slabs and its refill size halved (`lg_fill_div`), or its refill size
   doubled if it ran empty since the last GC. In the soak this comes to
   about 100 fills and 100 flushes per bin per second (its `nfills` and
   `nflushes` counters, section 5), each moving a few slots from a high
   slab to `slabcur`, the lowest-address non-full slab. challoc's GC runs
   every 10 s (`tcache_gc_interval_ms`) and only from `tick`, which only
   runs on a slow path (every 64th refill or flush). Under the soak's
   balanced DEL:SET churn the cache's length hardly moves (the churn probe
   below shows it does not reach either boundary at all under a strict
   alternation), so the slow path is not reached, the GC never runs, and
   every freed slot is reused in place by the next allocation of the same
   class.
3. **Refill and flush selection.** Both allocators refill from the lowest
   non-full slab. jemalloc's flush returns the *oldest* cached slots; the
   loop's step 3 (dacbedd) returns the *highest-addressed* ones. jemalloc
   also lowers `slabcur` to any lower-addressed slab that a free reopens
   (`arena_bin_lower_slab`), which challoc's `plan` matches by always
   scanning `partial.lowest_set()`.

The consequence for the delete phase, measured directly this time with a
one-class churn probe (`bench/probes/churn.c`, results in the counters
file): 30000 blocks of one class, a random 56% freed, then a strict
free-one/allocate-one churn at 19 M ops/s for 20 s.

- challoc with its default 128-slot cache: 469 slabs at 44.6% before, 469
  slabs at 44.6% after. With an 8-slot cache: the same. **The cache's
  length never moves under a balanced churn, so it never reaches empty or
  full, no refill and no flush ever run, and every allocation gets back the
  slot that was just freed.** The flush policy (oldest or highest address,
  i.e. the dacbedd experiment) is irrelevant because the flush does not
  run; the soak of this review confirms it on the real workload (section 5).
- challoc with `tcache:false`: 469 -> 208 slabs at 99.9% occupancy within
  three seconds, resident memory halved after the decay window. The
  address-ordered slab machinery compacts perfectly; the per-thread cache
  is the only thing that defeats it.
- jemalloc: its 4-region 1 KiB bin drains 4.7% in 20 s, its 32-region 640
  bin not at all. Its GC runs on an allocation-byte schedule regardless of
  the cache level, so it does force a few refills from the lowest slab per
  event, but with the refill size shrunk to one or two slots by
  `lg_fill_div` the migration is slow; most of jemalloc's post-delete
  advantage in the soak (131 -> 111 MiB over eight minutes) is that
  migration plus 4-region slabs that empty by chance.

So the residue is not slab geometry, not the flush order, and not the decay
window: it is LIFO reuse in place, which no cache size and no boundary
policy changes. What fixes it is making some allocations *not* take the
just-freed slot: a scheduled fraction of pops must be served from the
lowest-address cached slot (or the refill side) instead of the top of the
stack, and the displaced frees must go back to their slabs on a schedule
too. That is P1, and it is a different mechanism from jemalloc's, which
only happens to compact because its GC empties the bin now and then.

### 3.3 TLS layout and dependent-load depth (design 6.1, 6.3)

`malloc`: GOT -> TLS slot -> `heap.caches[c]` -> `cache.len` -> slot, five
dependent loads (jemalloc: the tsd lives *in* the static TLS block, so its
bin header is `fs:[GOT offset + bin offset]` with no pointer load, and the
slot follows: three). `free`: `p -> c` is arithmetic, then `class_base[c]`
and `CLASSES[c]` (dense, hot), then `tables[c]` (three fields on one line),
then the descriptor word, then `heap.caches[c]` -> header -> push. The
annotation says the dependent depth costs little: at one thread challoc is
2% behind on aarch64 and 7% ahead on x86_64 with 4-5% more instructions per
operation than jemalloc. What does show is *where* the header lives: 44% of
`malloc`'s cycles wait for the per-class cache header line, which is one of
52 separately allocated metadata objects per thread (each `ClassCache` is
its own 8 KiB metadata block), so a thread that touches many classes has 52
header lines on 52 pages, plus 52 descriptor pages (the tables are staggered
by design so that they do not alias in one L1/L2 set, which also puts each on
its own page). jemalloc's 36 bin headers are 1.5 KiB of one struct. That is
the 2x L1 dTLB refill rate on aarch64 (3.4 vs 1.7 per operation at one
thread; the V2 has a 48-entry L1 dTLB, and each operation touches the block,
a cache header page, a descriptor page and the heap) and it is worth about
1.5 cycles per operation there; on the Xeon the 96-entry L1 dTLB and cheap
STLB hits hide it (no walks at one thread). Moving the per-class stack tops
into the TLS block, the standing Phase 1.5 candidate, would remove one
dependent load; putting the 52 headers side by side inside `Heap` gets most
of that and all of the page spread without a TLS-block size change (P3).

### 3.4 Metadata placement and slab geometry (design 3, 4)

- Descriptor stagger (`desc_stagger`, 384 bytes per class) fixed a real L2
  set conflict on 2026-09-16 and the counters show no residue of it: L2
  misses per operation at one thread are identical to jemalloc's (0.042 vs
  0.043). Keep it.
- 64 KiB minimum slabs (64..8192 slots per slab) are what makes refills and
  flushes cheap: one lock hold and a handful of descriptor lines per 512
  slots. The 16 KiB trial halved xmalloc-test for exactly that reason. The
  counters do not argue for smaller slabs; only the RSS floor does, and only
  if P1 is not enough (P6).
- `ClassState` 640-byte stride, `SlabTable` 384-byte stride, 128-byte
  padding: none of these lines appear in the annotations. Fine.
- mimalloc's 0.17 L1 dTLB refills per operation (against challoc's 3.9 and
  jemalloc's 1.9) come from 2 MiB huge pages on its arenas (`madvise`
  THP), not from layout cleverness. challoc's `thp` tunable is off by
  default for the fork copy-on-write reason in design 7; that decision is
  right for Valkey and Redis and should stay, but P8 notes the opt-in.

### 3.5 Purge policy and the background thread (design 8)

Not on any hot path and not visible in any profile: the decay driver takes
no lock unless a purge is due, the batch goes out through
`process_madvise`, and the background thread sleeps on a futex. The soak's
`pdirty: 0` readings mean nothing is `Empty` to purge, so purge policy is
not where the residue is. One design fact matters for P1: Valkey turns the
background thread on, and while it is present the slow path does no purge
work; a cache GC driven from the fast path must not assume a background
thread either way.

### 3.6 Huge allocations (design 7)

One lock, hash-table extents, first fit by address; none of the benchmarks
exercise it and Valkey's large values (> 256 KiB) are rare. No change
proposed. The `Huge::prepare` commit bug found on the VMs (7e3befd) is the
only huge-path item in the recent history and is fixed.

## 4. Proposals

Each: mechanism, counter evidence, expected effect on throughput and on
post-delete RSS, risk and blast radius, verification.

### P1. Scheduled address-ordered pops and flushes (a cache that compacts)

Mechanism. Keep the LIFO stack for warmth, but every `2^k` pops of a class
(the `allocs` counter the pop already increments on the header line; `k`
around 3, i.e. one allocation in eight, tunable) serve the allocation from
the *lowest-address* cached slot instead of the top. To make that cheap the
cache keeps a sorted run at the bottom of the stack with a cursor: after a
refill the claimed slots are written ascending (today they are reversed so
the lowest pops first; instead leave them ascending and start the cursor at
0), and after a flush the kept half is ascending too (dacbedd's form). The
scheduled pop returns `slots[cursor]`, moves the top entry into that
position, shrinks `len` and advances the cursor; entries behind the cursor
are ordinary LIFO entries again and are never consulted for the minimum,
so no invariant is broken. When the cursor reaches the end of the run, the
scheduled pop claims a small batch from the lowest partial slab into a new
run (a refill that does not wait for the cache to be empty). All O(1) on
the fast path: one counter test and a well-predicted branch, taken once in
`2^k` calls. On the free side, every `2^k` pushes of a class run the
existing flush of the `cap / 2` highest-address entries even when the cache
is not full (the same counter test on `free`, or, cheaper, only when the
scheduled pop finds the cache more than half full). Net effect under a
balanced churn: one allocation in `2^k` lands in the lowest slab that has
room, the displaced free goes back to its (higher) slab within a few hundred
operations, and the live set migrates downward at `1/2^k` of the allocation
rate: at Valkey's 30k operations per second per class that compacts a
13k-region residue in seconds, where jemalloc's GC takes minutes. Keep the
10 s timer GC as the idle backstop and the `low_water` shrink as is.

Evidence. Section 3.2 and the churn probe: with the cache off the same
class compacts from 469 to 208 slabs in three seconds; with any cache it
does not move at all; the flush order does not matter because the flush
never runs. The probe is the acceptance test for the prototype: at
`k = 3` the 469 slabs must fall toward 208 within a few seconds of churn.

Expected effect. Throughput: -1..-3% on larson (one in eight allocations
returns a cold slot instead of the just-freed warm one; the scheduled flush
is a sort of 128 pointers per 1024 frees, already paid for once per 512
today). xmalloc-test: neutral (its caches are always at a boundary and the
producer never re-touches the slot). Post-delete RSS: this is the mechanism
that removes the residue rather than shrinking it; with the compaction
complete, challoc's post-delete RSS is bounded by live data plus one partial
slab per class plus the caches, which is below jemalloc's 47..55%
occupancies, so the 10% criterion should be met with margin. `k` trades
throughput for compaction speed and can be set per class (the 640..1024
classes matter to Valkey; the 16..64 classes do not).

Risk and blast radius. `heap.rs`: `pop`, `push_cache`, `flush`, `refill`
and the `ClassCache` header; the zero prefix for `calloc` goes away for the
kept run (dacbedd already drops it). No change to slabs, bitmaps, locks,
stats or the API. The invariant to test on the host: after a flush and
after a refill the stack is ascending below the top, and a scheduled pop
returns the minimum of the cache. Interacts with Valkey's defragmenter
only positively (a compacting cache makes `frag_smallbins` fall on its
own).

Verification. larson/xmalloc/alloc-test rows on both VMs before and after
(CLAUDE.md rule for `heap.rs`); the churn probe under both allocators; the
20-minute soak on the arm64 VM with the per-class occupancy sampler of this
review; the criterion is the plan's.

### P2. Thread-affine slab ownership

Two forms, the second being the fallback:

(a) *Affinity, not ownership.* `plan` prefers a slab this heap claimed from
last (a per-`(heap, class)` hint slab number, one `u32` in the cache header)
when it is still partial, and only then falls back to the class's lowest
partial slab. Slabs stay class-owned and remote frees stay as they are; what
changes is that consecutive refills of one thread come from one slab and
two threads stop interleaving runs in the same slab. The hint costs nothing
on the fast paths.

(b) *`narenas` default above 1* (e.g. `min(4 * ncpus, 64)` like jemalloc,
assigned round robin per heap as `Arenas::assign` already does). Measured
today: closes the 10-thread gap (117 vs 114 cycles per operation), +6
instructions per operation for the `multi_arena` check and routing, and
keeps xmalloc-test's remote-free advantage because frees route by the
descriptor's arena into the freeing thread's per-arena cache.

Evidence. Section 2.4 and 3.1: the whole 10-thread gap is coherence on
shared slab lines (+0.7 L1D and +0.05 L2 misses per operation), removed by
`narenas:16`.

Expected effect. Throughput: +4.5% larson at 10 threads on aarch64 and
+7..10% on x86_64 (to within 2.4% and 2% of jemalloc respectively, measured
with `narenas:16`); the 16-thread row on x86_64 should move more. RSS:
(a) neutral; (b) worse by up to one partial slab per class per arena, and
the per-arena split changes what the defragmenter sees: Valkey reads its bin
statistics through `MALLCTL_ARENAS_ALL` (challoc's `<i> = 4096` sums every
arena, task 5), but `experimental.utilization.batch_query` answers the
bin-wide `nfree`/`nregs` for the pointer's arena, and each arena keeps its
own partial set and empty list, so the Valkey defrag tests must be rerun
under (b). (a) has none of that.

Risk and blast radius. (a): `class.rs` `plan` and one header field; the
first-fit property becomes "lowest partial slab among the slabs this thread
is not already using", which slightly weakens address-ordered compaction
under churn (a thread keeps filling its hint slab instead of the global
lowest) and must be measured with P1's soak. (b): a config default; the
code exists and is tested, but design 13.3 says "not recommended", the
per-arena set tables cost about 1 MiB and 312 VMAs per arena, and the
defragmenter's per-bin view changes.

Verification. larson 1/10/16 threads, xmalloc-test, alloc-test on both VMs;
the Valkey `unit/memefficiency` suite; the soak with P1.

Conflicts. With the address-ordered policy that P1 and P5 rely on (a keeps
it per thread; b keeps it per arena).

### P3. Dense cache headers inside `Heap`, one slot block per heap

Mechanism. Replace `caches: [*mut ClassCache; NCLASSES]` with an inline
`[CacheHeader; NCLASSES]` (len, cap, low_water, zeroed, allocs, moved: 32
bytes, 1.6 KiB for 52 classes, 13 lines) and one lazily grown slot block per
heap whose per-class offsets come from the capacity ladder (a static table,
so `slots = heap.block + OFFSET[c] + len * 8` needs no pointer load). The
sentinel-cache trick becomes `cap == 0` in the inline header. The `others`
list for foreign arenas keeps the current shape.

Evidence. Section 2.3: 44% of `malloc`'s cycles wait for the header line;
section 3.3: 52 header pages and the aarch64 L1 dTLB refill rate (3.4 vs 1.7
per operation at one thread).

Expected effect. Throughput: +1..3% single-threaded (one dependent load
fewer, 52 fewer pages in the working set; the same shape as jemalloc's tsd
bins), more on aarch64 than x86_64. RSS: none (metadata only; slot blocks
committed lazily per class as now).

Risk and blast radius. `heap.rs` only, but most of it: `ensure_cache`,
`gc`, `flush_all`, `stats`, explicit caches (`tcache.create` is a detached
heap and keeps working), thread exit. The `Heap` grows from 512 bytes to
about 2 KiB.

Verification. larson 1-thread rows on both VMs (this is the row that
resolves 2% with five rounds); dTLB refills per operation on aarch64 should
fall toward jemalloc's 1.7.

### P4. Metadata on 2 MiB pages, or interleaved descriptor rows

Mechanism. `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)` on the committed prefix of the metadata
region (heaps, caches, descriptor tables), the equivalent of jemalloc's
`metadata_thp:auto`, probed at runtime like every other feature; THP on
metadata does not have the fork copy-on-write concern that user memory has
(the metadata working set is small). Alternative without THP: lay the 52
descriptor tables out as rows indexed by slab number so that slab `k` of
every class shares a page (costs committed memory proportional to the
busiest class's slab count times 52; not recommended).

Evidence. Section 3.3: 2x L1 dTLB refills on aarch64, roughly 1.5 cycles per
operation at one thread; none measurable on x86_64.

Expected effect. 0..2% on aarch64, ~0 on x86_64; no RSS change beyond THP
rounding of the metadata prefix (2 MiB granularity).

Risk. Low: `meta.rs` commit path and a probe. Verify with the aarch64 dTLB
column.

### P5. The compacting flush of dacbedd

Keep the *form* (release the highest-address half, keep the lowest half
ascending) because P1 needs exactly that shape, but do not expect anything
from it on its own: the soak of this review (section 5) and the churn probe
show the layout does not move with it, because under a balanced churn the
flush never runs. Throughput was measured neutral on both VMs on 2026-09-16
apart from -2.2% xmalloc-test on x86_64 (the sort of 1024 instead of 512
pointers); that sort is also what P1's scheduled flush would pay. The
`PROGRESS.md` "In progress" entry for acceptance step 3 should be closed
with this reading rather than left as an open experiment.

### P6. 4..8-region slabs for the 512..1024-byte classes

Only if P1 leaves the criterion unmet. The 2026-09-16 data prices it: -2.2%
(aarch64) / -3.8% (x86_64) larson for the two-regime 16 KiB form, and the
1024 class needs 4 KiB slabs to match jemalloc's 86% end occupancy. It needs
`MAX_PICKS` and `Reopen` to grow (a 128-slot refill spans 32 slabs) and a
page-group commit that already exists dormant. Conflicts with P2's gain.

### P7. Cache capacity ladder shaped by regions per slab

Mechanism. `cap = clamp(k * nslots_per_slab_fraction, MIN_CAP, max)` is not
meaningful with 64-slot slabs; the simpler jemalloc-compatible rule is to cap
classes above 512 bytes at 32..64 slots unless `malloc_conf` raises them.
Evidence: section 3.2 item 1. Effect: small direct RSS gain (a few hundred
KiB per thread in Valkey), refill/flush lock work up by the same factor, so
measure xmalloc-test (64-byte class untouched) and larson (its 512..1000-byte
allocations would see 4x the refills). Do this together with P1, whose
adaptive `lg_fill_div` makes the static cap matter less.

### P8. `thp:always` for slab regions as an opt-in

mimalloc's numbers (0.17 dTLB refills per operation, 5.6 L1D misses per
operation from its in-band free list) show what huge pages buy and what
they do not; challoc's `thp` tunable exists and is documented off. Make sure
it does `MADV_HUGEPAGE` on slab commit when set, record a benchmark row, and
leave the default. Not a Phase 1 item.

### Not proposed

- Removing the bitmap check from `free` (design's security property): the
  counters say its cost at one thread is invisible and at ten threads it is
  the sharing of the word, which P2 removes.
- Moving stack tops into the TLS block: P3 gets the header line and the
  pages without changing the TLS model; revisit only if P3's 1-thread rows
  still trail jemalloc on aarch64.
- Per-CPU mode (design 15): would also end the sharing, but P2(a) does it
  without rseq.

## 5. Soak evidence

`bench/results/2026-09-17-gce-review-soak.md`: 600 s protocol (300 s write,
300 s delete, 60 s settle) on the arm64 VM, `MEMORY MALLOC-STATS` sampled
every 20 s, challoc at `dacbedd` (compacting flush) against bundled jemalloc
5.3.0.

| | challoc | jemalloc |
|---|---:|---:|
| RSS after settle (MiB) | 161.7 | 134.3 (+20.4%) |
| peak RSS (MiB) | 568.6 | 576.8 |
| throughput, both phases | 3..5% behind | |
| class 640 slabs, end of defrag -> end | 365 -> 362 at 36% | 972 -> 900 at 42% -> 45% (32 regions) |
| class 768 | 354 -> 352 at 44% | 1840 -> 1581 at 45% -> 51% (16 regions) |
| class 896 | 410 -> 409 at 44% | 936 -> 872 at 44% -> 46% (32 regions) |
| class 1024 | 480 -> 478 at 44% | 6367 -> 4037 at 52% -> 81% (4 regions) |
| class 320 | 78 -> 76 at 42% | 227 -> 218 at 44% -> 45% (64 regions) |

Reading:

- The compacting flush does not move the residue: 240 s of memtier DEL:SET
  churn at 1.2 M ops/s over a constant 104k keys changed challoc's slab
  counts by 0..3 per class. The churn probe explains why (the flush never
  runs under a balanced churn), so the experiment of acceptance step 3 is
  answered: negative, for a reason that also rules out every other
  flush-selection policy.
- jemalloc's drain is a function of regions per slab under its GC-forced
  ~100 fills and flushes per bin per second: 4-region slabs reach 81%,
  8-region 67%, 16-region 51%, 32-region 46%, 64-region flat. challoc's
  classes have 64..102 regions per slab, so a jemalloc-shaped GC alone
  would leave them where jemalloc's 64-region bin is: flat. This is the
  argument for P1's stronger mechanism (a scheduled fraction of allocations
  taken from the lowest cached address, which migrates live data regardless
  of slab size) over both the compacting flush and a jemalloc-style GC, and
  against spending P6's throughput on smaller slabs first.
- The +20% is entirely the 640..1024 classes: the same ~13.3k live regions
  per class sit in 22..30 MiB of challoc slabs at 36..44% against 15.8 MiB
  (1024), 18.5 MiB (768), 17.6 MiB (640) and 23.9 MiB (896) of jemalloc
  slabs. Full compaction of those four classes (P1's target) would put
  challoc at roughly 100 MiB after settle, below jemalloc.
- The soak's throughput and p99 rows (3..5% behind in every phase) are the
  single-arena sharing cost of section 3.1 seen from Valkey: its main
  thread, bio threads and the load generators share slabs. P2 should
  narrow them.

The control run with the pre-compacting-flush library (8df225f, oldest half
flushed), same protocol and VM, gives 161.5 MiB after settle against 161.7
with the compacting flush, the same 37..44% occupancies and the same slab
counts to within a few slabs (end of the soak results file). The two flush
policies are indistinguishable on the real workload, which closes the
acceptance step 3 experiment.

## 6. Sequencing and what to put in `docs/plan.md`

Order of work, each step gated by its own measurement:

1. **Close acceptance step 3 as measured.** Record in PROGRESS.md that the
   compacting flush does not move the residue (section 5 and the churn
   probe), keep its form, and stop treating it as the open experiment.
   Commit `bench/probes/churn.c` and its driver as the standing regression
   probe for cache compaction.
2. **P1, the compacting cache.** Prototype in an A/B build, judge with the
   churn probe first (469 -> ~208 slabs within seconds at `k = 3`; try
   `k = 2..5`), then the larson/xmalloc/alloc-test rows on both VMs, then
   the 20-minute soak on the arm64 VM with the occupancy sampler. This is
   the Phase 1 acceptance item; nothing else in this list is needed to pass
   it, and P6 (small slabs) should not be tried before it.
3. **P2(a), thread-affine refills.** Independent of P1 in code (`plan`
   and a hint field) and the larson 10-thread fix; measure with the
   `narenas` A/B as the reference (challoc `narenas:16` is the number to
   reach). If (a) does not reach it, (b) `narenas` default is the fallback,
   with the Valkey defrag suite rerun. This is the Phase 1.5 acceptance item
   ("larson within 10% on both VMs").
4. **P3, dense cache headers.** After P1 and P2 have landed (it rewrites
   the same file), for the 1-thread aarch64 residue; measure with the
   1-thread larson row and the aarch64 dTLB column.
5. **P7 and P4** as small follow-ups behind P1 and P3 respectively; **P6**
   only if the P1 soak leaves the criterion unmet; **P8** as a documented
   opt-in whenever convenient.

Proposed plan text (a new Phase 1 acceptance task list replacing "step 3",
and one Phase 1.5 task replacing task 4):
