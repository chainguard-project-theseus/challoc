  Mechanism: the gap is system time, not user time (user 13.3 vs 13.5 ms; sys 7.8 vs 6.2 ms), and challoc's own initialisation is not it (`/bin/true` under challoc costs the same 1 ms as plain; jemalloc's init is the one that shows). It is the commit path: challoc reserves every size-class region `PROT_NONE` and makes slabs readable/writable one at a time as the class first needs them, and `node -e 0` touches about 40 size classes while deserialising the V8 snapshot, so it issues about 270 more `mprotect` calls than glibc (93 x 4 KiB, 41 x 8 KiB, 35 x 64 KiB, ...; each one splits or merges a VMA in the class region) and takes about 880 more first-touch minor faults (3049 vs 2167: one fresh page per slab across many isolated regions instead of glibc's contiguous `brk` heap, whose pages are faulted in runs). About 2 ms of a 20 ms process, and the price of the layout that keeps allocator metadata and each class out of the others' reach (README, "Address space layout"); the same run is 7 MiB (14%) smaller in RSS than glibc. Where it would be attacked: the commit path of `src/class.rs` (lane B): committing a class's first slabs in larger groups, or using `MAP_FIXED` `mmap` over the reservation instead of `mprotect` where the kernel merges VMAs. No change proposed by this lane (measurement only).
- No other row: `json_churn` is within 0.5% of glibc and jemalloc on time and 10% smaller than glibc in RSS; the server row's req/s and p99 are within 2.2% of glibc and ahead of jemalloc, and its RSS (peak, final, VmHWM) is below every other allocator's. The end-of-load mapping table places challoc's 108.9 MiB final RSS as 37.0 MiB `challoc:huge` (the 1 MiB `Buffer.allocUnsafe` backing stores, retained up to `huge_retain_max`, 64 MiB by default), 6.6 MiB `challoc:classes`, 2.0 MiB `challoc:meta`, the rest node's own text and V8's mmaps.
