### RSS attribution

The three failing rows are the 16-thread rows whose RSS was inside the band at
166a464 and whose growth coincides with task 4c (`narenas` default
`min(4 * ncpus, 64)` with least-loaded assignment, 8408d72): in the A/B files
the `-w 10` xmalloc row went from 39 MiB (`old`, every 2026-09-17 file before
4c) to 85 MiB (`new` in `2026-09-17-aarch64-gce-narenas-default.md` and every
file since), larson16 from 135 to 185 MiB, and the 4c notes already recorded
alloc-testN at +31%. Tasks 4e (sized frees), 4f (dense bitmap) and 5 (dense
headers) did not move these rows (dense-bitmap A/B: xmalloc 85.4 -> 85.4 MiB,
larson16 183 -> 187 MiB). Mechanism: with 16 live threads each thread has its
own arena, and each arena holds its own partial slab per class it touches
(challoc slabs are 4..64 KiB against jemalloc's 4..20 KiB bins) plus its own
dirty slabs until the decay interval; mstress and xmalloc-test are the
cross-thread producer/consumer shapes where remote frees keep many slabs of
many arenas partially populated at once. The probe below (one run per
variant, `MALLOC_CONF=narenas:N` against the default and jemalloc) is the
direct test of that reading.

Probe (`~/logs/rss-probe.sh` on each VM after the scorecards, the Phase 0
command lines at 16 threads, one run each, `maxrss_kb` from `bench/rt`, MiB;
"vs je" is the default column against jemalloc, the band is 10%):

VMs (xmalloc-test -61% / -63%, mstress +5.4% / +5.3%, alloc-test +0.5% /

from 16 arenas up (16 threads take 16 arenas under the default's least-loaded
assignment) the rows read as in the protocol run. The 4c decision that bought
larson16 its +11.9% -> +3.2% traded these rows for it; the throughput side of
that trade at 4 arenas (and at 8) is not measured yet (larson's throughput
lines were not kept by this probe) and is the first A/B of the remediation.

Remediation candidates, for the next in-progress item (none applied here):

- arena count nearer the thread count is not a lever for these rows: 16
  threads occupy 16 arenas under any count of 16 or more, and `narenas:16`
  measured the same RSS as the default in the 4c A/B;
- releasing a heap's partial slabs to the class's shared partial list when
  the cache flushes (or bounding the number of partial slabs an arena may
  hold per class, jemalloc's `nonfull_slabs` heap serves the whole arena but
  its slabs are smaller), so cross-thread frees refill the freeing thread's
  next claim instead of leaving a mostly-empty slab in another arena;
- a shorter decay for dirty slabs of arenas that are not the freeing
  thread's own (mstress: 16 threads each allocating and freeing large
  batches; the peak is the sum of every arena's dirty slabs waiting for the
  same GC interval);
- releasing a heap's partial slabs at thread exit (the prompt's candidate)
  does not reach these rows, whose threads live for the whole run, but is
  right for thread-churn programs.

The A/B for whichever is tried is the Phase 0 set's three rows at 16 threads
plus larson1/10/16 and xmalloc `-w 10` for the throughput cost, the Valkey
soak's RSS columns, and the Ruby rows.

The SIGPROF sampler in the results files (`bench/profile.sh`, 20 s, 1000 Hz

aarch64 1375 challoc samples, `je_free` 71.5%, `je_malloc` 21.7%, 14 samples

Caveat on the literal reading for aarch64: Rust outlines aarch64 atomics into
`__aarch64_*` helpers (LSE detected at run time), so every atomic anywhere in
the allocator is a separate symbol and three of them are in the top 10 by
rank, at 0.16 / 0.08 / 0.04% of challoc's cycles. On x86_64 the same atomics
are inline `lock` instructions inside `claim` / `release_owned` / `dec_live`
and no symbol appears. The substance is the same on both: 0.3% of challoc's
cycles in atomics, all on the slab claim / release paths, none in `je_malloc`
or `je_free`, which is what the criterion is for. Recorded as PASS on
substance with the numbers here; if the human reads the criterion by rank,
aarch64 fails it by 0.16% and the fix is not allocator work (building with
`-C target-feature=+lse` inlines them and would drop pre-8.1 CPUs).

### Counter attribution of the remaining gap

one run per row and variant), `ROWS="larson1 larson10 larson16"
VARIANTS="new jemalloc"`, `new` = c4a0772. The script's larson op count was
wrong (larson prints `relative time = 1e9 / throughput`, so throughput x
relative time is always 1e9 and every row read 200.00 M ops/s; fixed in this

2026-09-17 reference method); the table below is the run's raw counters

(`dTLB-load-misses` is `L1D_TLB_REFILL` on Neoverse V2, mostly L2-TLB hits;
on the Xeon it is the STLB miss count, near zero for larson's working set.)

Reading. L1D misses per operation are at parity on aarch64 (2.22 vs 2.25 at
16 threads; 3.10 vs 2.22 at 10 threads in the 2026-09-17 reference, the
slab-sharing item tasks 4c/4f closed) and below jemalloc's on x86_64 at every
thread count. What remains is instructions: challoc runs 19..21 more
instructions per larson operation on aarch64 (+9%) and 29..30 more on x86_64
(+15%) than jemalloc, at every thread count. On aarch64 that is the whole
gap, +4.4 / +4.4 / +2.4 cycles per operation at 1 / 10 / 16 threads (+6.8%,
+5.7%, +3.1%; the Phase 0 row +3.2%), plus 2.3 extra L1 dTLB refills per
operation that mostly hit the L2 TLB (the two-page per-class metadata of 4f
and the region-per-class layout). On x86_64 the extra instructions are
hidden at 1 and 10 threads (cycles per operation equal, 72.2 vs 72.7 and
117.2 vs 116.7: the core has the issue width to spare) and surface at 16
threads, when both hyperthreads of every one of the 8 physical cores are
busy and share that width: +12.6 cycles per operation (+8.1% here, +5.9%
in the protocol row). That is the iteration-19/20 attribution
(hyperthread pairs) made concrete: the cost is the instruction count, and
the hyperthread pairing is what stops the core from hiding it. The
instruction delta is in `je_free` (the class/arena routing of a plain
free: region classify, owner-word load, home-or-other cache choice) and
`je_malloc`'s cache header path; a per-instruction annotate of both under
larson16 is the first step of any attempt on it.
