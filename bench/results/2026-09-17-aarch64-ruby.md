## Reading

- Time: challoc is within 5.1% of jemalloc on every row. The gaps are on the
  two shortest processes (startup +5.1%, i.e. 2 ms of a 41 ms `ruby -e 1`;
  gem_list +4.4%, 5 ms of 119) and hash_aref_str (+3.2%, spread 0.342..0.362
  against jemalloc's 0.344..0.350); the six interpreter rows are within 1%.
  erb_render, where every render grows its output String through realloc
  and the GC frees it, is 7.1% ahead of jemalloc (0.897 against 0.966 s;
  mimalloc 0.847, glibc 0.867).
- Max RSS: below jemalloc on every row but gc_churn (-4.2% .. -20.1%; the
  array rows -4.5% / -20.1%) and 5..13% above glibc on the small rows, a fixed
  0.8..1.5 MiB (metadata region and one committed slab per touched class),
  which jemalloc pays twice over.
- gc_churn, the row shaped like Ruby's GC (long-lived medium buffers, bursts
  of short-lived ones freed by sweep): peak RSS 383 MiB against jemalloc's
  359 (+6.6%), mimalloc's 354 and glibc's 316 (+21%: glibc calls
  `malloc_trim` after every full GC in the `--without-jemalloc` build and
  consolidates the brk top). After the 10 forced full GCs challoc, glibc and
  mimalloc give back about 2% (Ruby's own heap pages); jemalloc on this VM
  gives back 48% (359 -> 186 MiB). On the x86_64 VM jemalloc gives back 2.2%
  like the others: Wolfi's aarch64 jemalloc is built with 64 KiB pages
  (`Page size: 65536` from `MALLOC_CONF=stats_print:true`, 4096 on x86_64;
  the kernel page is 4 KiB on both VMs), so the difference is that build's
  run geometry and decay accounting, not the architecture. challoc's
  `dirty_decay_ms` (10 s) deadline is checked on the slow path, so a 1.6 s
  process purges nothing, and the `--with-jemalloc` Ruby never calls
  `malloc_trim`. Not a criterion (max RSS is within 10%), recorded as the
  one place challoc keeps more of a free burst than jemalloc does.
- Where challoc stands on Ruby's allocation shape: Ruby 3.4 keeps objects up
  to 640 bytes in its own mmap'ed slots, so malloc sees medium buffers
  (strings over 640 bytes, arrays over 78 elements, st_tables, iseq and
  method structures) with the GC freeing them in bursts. On the interpreter
  rows that shape costs challoc nothing against jemalloc (time within noise,
  lower max RSS); the fixed costs show on the 40 ms and 120 ms processes
  (1..2 ms of init); the burst-free row shows challoc holding 4..7% more of
  the burst than jemalloc at peak and, unlike the 64 KiB-page jemalloc, not
  returning it inside the process lifetime. All rows are within the task's
  10% band on time and max RSS; no regression to fix, the post-free residency
  is noted in PROGRESS.md for the human.
