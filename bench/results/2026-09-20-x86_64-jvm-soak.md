NMT (`-XX:NativeMemoryTracking=summary`, `jcmd <pid> VM.native_memory summary scale=KB`) is the JVM's own account of the native memory it asked for: "committed" counts the bytes the JVM malloc'd (with NMT's 16-byte header each) plus the pages it committed by mmap (Java heap, metaspace, code cache, thread stacks and GC structures). Rss minus NMT committed at the same instant is what the allocator adds on its own account (caching, size-class rounding, fragmentation, retained pages, metadata) less what the JVM committed but never touched (NMT counts mmap commits, not resident pages, so the gap understates the allocator by the same untouched amount under every variant). "malloc'd" is NMT's malloc total across categories: `Other` holds the direct ByteBuffers (`Unsafe.allocateMemory`), `Internal` the JVM's miscellany, `Thread` the stacks (touched pages) and per-thread arenas, `Class` metaspace, `Code` the code cache, `GC` the card table and remembered sets, `Compiler` the JIT arenas. NMT's own overhead (its header per malloc plus its tables, the last column) is the same bytes for every allocator, so the comparisons between allocators hold; the header does push a malloc(65536) to 65552 bytes, across a power-of-two class boundary in every size-class allocator (see the reading). jcmd is a second JVM started from the harness shell (no LD_PRELOAD) and wakes the server's attach listener (one more thread, the same for every variant).

## Reading

- The question this soak was built for (the human, 2026-09-20: the 5-minute
  server row with one buffer size never lets glibc's arenas fragment, and
  the JVM's biggest native consumers are mmapped by the JVM itself): does
  a growing and shrinking pool with mixed-size direct buffers separate the
  allocators? It does. Over 1200 s stock glibc grows from 1032 MiB at the
  end of the first window to 1260 at the end of the last (its low-window
  Rss climbs 1083, 1113, 1119, 1157, 1209, 1162, 1222, 1125, 1118, 1260;
  slope +2.19 MiB/min over the last 600 s) and jemalloc's low windows sit
  at 1105..1228 with slope +4.55; challoc, glibc-arena2 and glibc-trim do
  not grow (slopes -2.96, +0.20, -2.32). At the end glibc holds 563 MiB
  more than the JVM asked for (Rss 1260 against NMT committed 698), jemalloc
  339, challoc 294, glibc-arena2 200, glibc-trim 6. That last column is the
  number an operator fights, and it is where the allocators differ; NMT's
  own categories are within 1 MiB of each other for everything but `Other`
  (the live direct buffers at the instant of the sample, 82..199 MiB, GC
  timing) and the Java heap is the same 512 MiB under every variant.
- Criterion (a row more than 10% behind the best of glibc, glibc-trim and
  jemalloc on RSS, slope, req/s or p99 gets the mechanism named): challoc
  is behind glibc-trim on peak Rss (+15.5%: 1237 against 1070 MiB), final
  Rss (+24.5%: 1017 against 817) and tail mean (+16.2%: 1119 against 963),
  and behind glibc-arena2 (not in the criterion's set, the fleet's usual
  mitigation) by +21.1% / +3.3% / +13.6%. Ahead of stock glibc on all three
  (-1.9%, -19.3%, -3.6%) and of jemalloc (-5.5%, -11.8%, -6.6%). Slope:
  challoc's -2.96 MiB/min is the lowest of the five (glibc-trim -2.32,
  glibc-arena2 +0.20, glibc +2.19, jemalloc +4.55); a negative slope here
  is the last low window's decay still under way, not a trend, every
  allocator but glibc and jemalloc is flat from the second window on
  (challoc's high-window maxima are 1224, 1227, 1226, 1230, 1220, 1231,
  1221, 1233, 1228, 1231 MiB: no growth across ten cycles). req/s: within
  the band (json high windows -4.2..-4.6%, blob high -0.5..-3.3%, low
  windows -0.6..+6.6%), p99 within the band (json 4 ms against 3 in the
  high windows, ab's integer milliseconds; blob 49 against 49..50).
- Mechanism of the RSS gap against glibc-trim and glibc-arena2, from the
  numbers on record (the amd64 VM's raw soak output was erased by the
  server row's re-run before its stats print was read, harness bug fixed
  in 585e20b; the aarch64 file has the full print and the same shape):
  the final `/proc/<pid>/smaps` puts 442 MiB in challoc's named regions
  (`challoc:classes` 223.5, `challoc:huge` 191.1, `challoc:meta` 27.5)
  against 140.5 MiB the JVM had malloc'd at that instant (NMT, of which
  `Other`, the live direct buffers, 107.1), i.e. 294 MiB the allocator
  holds on its own account at the end of a low window: (1) dirty pages
  inside the 10 s decay window (`opt.dirty_decay_ms: 10000`, no background
  thread: `purge::decay` walks every arena and the huge allocator on any
  thread's periodic tick), the freed direct buffers of the last seconds
  waiting to be reused or purged, where glibc-trim's `malloc_trim(0)`
  every 5 s hands back every free page at once (its "allocator overhead"
  is 6 MiB) and glibc-arena2's two arenas simply keep a fixed 984 MiB from
  the second window on; (2) slab occupancy: the Cleaner frees the buffers
  of one GC in a burst and leaves every touched slab partly occupied by
  the survivors (on the aarch64 run challoc's own count is active 305 MB
  against allocated 171 MB, 56% occupancy, and the huge allocator held
  222 MiB of empty extents beside 75 MiB of live runs at the final
  sample). The shape is visible in the window table: challoc's Rss is
  1157..1220 MiB at the end of every high window and 946..1025 at the end
  of every low one, i.e. it returns about 200 MiB within the first 10 s of
  a lull (the series: 1236 MiB at 76 s, 1040 at 86 s) and then holds the
  level, while glibc-trim's low windows reach 679..912 MiB. Owner of the
  decay policy: lane B (`src/purge.rs`, `src/policy.rs`); nothing here
  changes `src/`.
- CPU per request: challoc is +7.2..+7.9% in the high windows (69.6 us
  against 64.5..64.9) and +2.6..+4.3% in the low ones (58.3 against
  55.9..56.8); inside the 10% band, attributed anyway with `sudo perf`
  on the VM host over a 10 s slice of a high window (HttpBench soak under
  each allocator, two `ab -c 128`, after 20 s of load; `.tmp-vm-perf-soak.sh`):
  challoc 263.8 G cycles, 169.6 G instructions, 750k minor faults, 87.9 s
  task-clock; glibc 256.9 G, 169.5 G, 379k, 84.7 s; glibc-trim 256.9 G,
  170.8 G, 776k, 84.8 s. Equal instructions, +2.7% cycles, twice glibc's
  page faults (the freed direct-buffer pages challoc purged with
  MADV_DONTNEED coming back on the next `direct.put`: the aarch64 print
  counts 198710 eager purges, 204.5 GB over the run, 170 MB/s); the
  allocator's own code is 0.87% of samples under challoc and glibc's
  malloc is inside libc.so.6's 6.8% (memcpy included), so the difference
  is stall cycles spread over the JIT code (39.4% against 40.8%) and the
  kernel (43.0% against 42.4%), not allocator instructions. glibc-trim
  pays the same faults (776k) at glibc's task-clock, so faults alone do
  not explain the 3.2 s; nothing further is named.
- The pool did not reach 256 threads: the largest pool size was 90..122
  (`/stats`) and the peak thread count 121..157 (`/proc/<pid>/status`),
  because the blob generator was the limit in the high windows (ab's
  single thread at 99% of a core moving 4.2 GiB/s of log-uniform bodies
  through 128 keep-alive connections; the json generator at 82..83%), so
  the server saw fewer than 256 requests in flight. The pool still cycled
  16 -> ~100 -> 16 every two minutes for the whole run (the Threads row:
  51..58 at the end of every window, the pool's 2 s keep-alive having
  retired the extra threads before the sample, `largest` 90..122), which is
  the thread churn the soak wanted; a multi-process generator would raise
  the ceiling.
- jemalloc's slope (+4.55 MiB/min) is the highest; its low windows drift
  1105, 1204, 1137, 1228, 1216, 1102, 1200, 1203, 1221, 1152 MiB; the
  4 KiB-page build here (the aarch64 file has the 64 KiB-page build's
  far larger figures).
- Everything ran in full: 1200 s per allocator, five allocators, 6318 s
  for the file; load average 6.8 at the start (the previous server row
  had just ended) and 7.5 at the end.

## Verdict: is it really better than glibc?

Against glibc as shipped, yes, on every RSS figure of this soak: peak
1237 against 1260 MiB, tail mean 1119 against 1161, 294 against 563 MiB
held beyond what the JVM asked for at the end, and no growth where glibc
gains 2.2 MiB/min over the last ten minutes; against jemalloc likewise
(1237 against 1308 peak, 294 against 339, flat against +4.6 MiB/min).
Against glibc tuned the way a good Java image ships it, no: glibc-trim's
tail mean is 16% below challoc's (963 against 1119 MiB) and its final Rss
25% below (817 against 1017), glibc-arena2's 14% and 3%, both at equal
req/s and 7..8% less server CPU per request in the high windows. The gap
is the ~300 MiB challoc holds in dirty pages inside its 10 s decay window
and in half-occupied slabs at the end of every lull, against a
`malloc_trim(0)` every 5 s that returns everything.
