# Verus TODO: bugs found and theorem candidates

*The bug list and theorem candidates of the Verus effort's `verus/TODO.md`, written for the version of the proofs in wolfi-dev/challoc#138 (the tracking PR of the series) and refreshed against main `563886e6`: notes marked "On main" say where a done theorem differs on main. Each entry otherwise keeps the names and the open items of the commit it was written at, so a name it gives or an item it calls open may differ on main. Open work on main is tracked in wolfi-dev/challoc#397.*

## Bugs verification found

- [#1](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/1), a batch query length wrap and out-of-bounds write; [#2](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/2), an abort sink UTF-8 split; [#3](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/3), a cpuset count overflow; and [#4](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/4), a per-CPU `push_batch` zero-prefix error, were merged.
- [#5](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/5), an x86_64 pop that read the zero prefix outside its rseq section, and [#6](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/6), its model, were approved and then closed when upstream removed the per-CPU mode.
- [#71](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/71), a report sink UTF-8 split, was merged; the sink’s character-boundary cut (`api::print::char_cut`) is verified.

A background purge wake-up could also be lost at startup: `start` published `PRESENT` after the thread’s first scan, and a free in that window did not kick the thread, which could then sleep without a timeout. [#72](https://github.com/chainguard-dev/challoc/pull/72), merged, has `start` kick the thread once it is published.

## Theorem candidates

These candidates would extend the guarantees beyond the theorems in [THEOREMS.md](THEOREMS.md) (added by wolfi-dev/challoc#422). Those marked "(done, theorem N)" are theorem N there, proved as narrowly as its title there says (theorem 16 is partly premised); bad input, "(done, the B column)", is the unnumbered "Bad input" section and the B column of the interface contract.

- **No syscall on the fast path** (done, theorem 8): Every function that can enter the kernel requires the opaque capability `sys::may_syscall()`: the raw syscall seams, every trusted body that reaches the kernel, and their callers: about 404 contracts on the integration branch (385 at the lane commit; the live-block `_block` cores and the functions of theorems 13, 15 and 16 that reach the kernel added the rest, with `Set::ensure` and `Inner::ensure_sets`, which lacked it). Only `extern "C"` entry points and `abort_fmt` grant it, and a test checks that in the source. The verification-only `theorem_no_syscall_on_fast_paths` calls the `malloc`, `free`, `free_sized` and `challoc_free_usable` fast paths without it, so they make no system call. A counter or token threaded through the slow paths would have changed hundreds of signatures. An `opens_invariants none` mask would have needed no new requires, but vstd specifies `Option::map`, `?` and the atomics with mask `any`. Still open: the `calloc` lookup before its pop (`api::shared` and `heap_for_thread` may initialise or create a heap), `realloc`, and `free_aligned_sized`'s hint, which the witness does not call.
- **Fast paths take no lock and terminate** (done, theorem 9): Formerly "the fast path never hangs". No function the fast branches of `malloc`, `free`, `free_sized`/`sdallocx` (flags 0) and `challoc_free_usable` call can acquire a lock, and each terminates. Each fast-path function declares an invariant mask (`opens_invariants none`, or the bitmap words' namespace for the unsized free's bit test) and `RawLock::lock`/`Lock::lock` declare `any`, so Verus's mask check rules out any call chain from them to a lock; that the entry points' fast branches call only those functions is read off their bodies. No `exec_allows_no_decreases_clause` was on these paths (the eleven left are all slow-path waits). The paths are loop-free apart from the trusted `cached_recently` (at most three compares); the step bound is read off the code, since Verus proves termination but has no cost model. vstd gives `?`, `Option::map`, the array-to-slice coercion and `core` atomics the default mask `any`, so eight `?`s became `let ... else`, one `map` a `match`, and four array reads and the `multi_arena` load go through `vspec::slice::array_get_unchecked` and `vspec::atomic` (since upstream #78 the route bit is in the `free_flags` byte, `Shared::free_flags`, since package FFLAGS verified over the trusted `vspec::flags::load`, and `vspec::atomic` is gone), and the owner-word load through arena isolation's `load_owner` (machine code identical on x86_64). Still open: `calloc` and `realloc` (`api::shared()` may wait on init; `heap_for_thread` may create a heap), `free_aligned_sized` and flagged `sdallocx` (`class_for` is unmasked), and a step count Verus can check (theorem 45 measures one at machine level on the pinned binary).
- **Fast-path cost bounds** (done, theorem 45; machine level, pinned build): over the rustc 1.98.1 x86_64 release build, `scripts/perf-budget.sh` enumerates every fast path of `malloc`, `free`, `free_sized`, `sdallocx` and `challoc_free_usable` in the disassembly and checks its maximum instructions, explicit loads and stores, conditional branches and 64-byte lines against `verus/perf-budget.txt` (`malloc` 34/11/5, 5 lines; `free` 51/18/2, 7 lines, routed 62/22/2, 9 lines; `free_sized` 40/13/2; `sdallocx` 42/13/2), that the fast paths have no back-edge, and that the bodies hold no atomic read-modify-write, fence, call or syscall. Open: aarch64 (the walker reads x86_64 only), Isla footprints to certify the load/store classification, and the remaining seed candidates P5-P10 (uncontended lock cost, scan length, scheduled-pop frequency, `flag_sort`, claim scans, slow-path lock holds), which are source-level. On main since wolfi-dev/challoc#423: the q16 and q8 tables measured with rustc 1.98.1 at `98790244`, checked by the `perf-budget` check; the "any" rows of the frees are below #138's after the double-free scan's rewrite (wolfi-dev/challoc#447) and later changes (`free` 67/22/2, 9 lines, in #138 85/25/2, 11 lines); aarch64 stays open work in wolfi-dev/challoc#397.
- **Decisions independent of user memory** (done, theorem 46): reading a user block's bytes takes the opaque capability `vspec::user::may_read_user()`, required by `UserMem::lemma_valid_reads` and `copy_user` and so by `class::verify::read_word`, `Huge::read_run_word` and `libc::copy_moved`; it is granted only by the three realloc cores before their copy and by `purge::check_zero()`'s `true`, which `api::tests::user_reads_only_at_grants` checks in the source. Open: the trusted bodies are covered by a reading of their code, not a proof; TLS placed in a user block (a program-allocated thread stack) is outside the statement. On main the guard test is `sys::tests::user_read_grants_only_in_listed_callers` (`src/sys/mod.rs`), which checks the grant sites; it does not check that `may_read_user` appears in an `ensures` only at `check_zero` and the grant (wolfi-dev/challoc#397).
- **No leak on thread exit** (done, theorem 10): When the destructor runs; that every heap gets one is not proved (with an invalid thread key, `ThreadKey::set` registers nothing and the heap is still created). `Heap::flush_all`, which `destroy` runs first, ensures every home and `others`-node header empty (`Heap::drained`) and a ghost released log (`CachePerms::released`) extended by exactly the `(class, address)` pairs those headers held (`Heap::held_all`); `Heap::flush` states the same per cache for any `n`, and `Shared::release_routed` now ensures it took every batch token out of the bag through `release_owned`. Ghost only; machine code unchanged. `destroy` itself is verified since package P10 (it frees through `free_caches_in`, so its headers' permissions stay current); still open: make "`toks` holds no token past `len`" and "every owned header is a home or node header" invariants of `Heap::wf`, and add `no_duplicates` to the log's new entries from theorem 5.
- **Shuffle is a permutation** (done, theorem 11): `shuffle_zero` ensures `permutes` (the multiset of the batch is unchanged) for every `rng` value, and `theorem_shuffle_permutation` derives equal counts, membership and duplicate-freedom. Randomness is out of scope. Still open: name the pre-shuffle batch in `claim_random_zero`'s `ensures` (needs a ghost return value).
- **Zero-on-free** (done, theorem 12): In release-time form. With `zero_on_free` set, every free path gives up a slot (cache push or bitmap release) or a huge run (`free_huge`) only while holding a `Zeroed` token for its bytes (a slot's class size; for a run, whole pages from its start as `Huge::spec_run_usable` admits, which the code makes its whole usable size): `zero_slot` and `zero_huge` return the token (through `write_zero`, machine code unchanged), and `heap::zero::theorem_zero_on_free` and `theorem_zero_on_free_huge` are discharged at each release in `Heap::free_slow_in`, `heap::free_direct` and `Heap::free_huge`; the fast pushes provably decline under `PUSH_ZERO`. Still open: hand `zero_huge` the run token the freeing path holds, so `Huge::usable_size` names the descriptor's usable pages and the huge token provably covers them all; make release coverage mechanical (a ghost `Zeroed` argument on `Shared::free_direct`, `Shared::free_huge` and `push_cache`); carry the token with the slot (per cached entry above the zero prefix and per clear bitmap bit), so a pop or claim of a slot freed under `zero_on_free` states it zero at hand-out. On main `write_zero` is gone: `zero_slot` zeroes through `vspec::user::zero_user`, and fresh memory through `vspec::zero::zero_raw`.
- **Fork-handler lock handoff** (done, theorem 13): Formerly "fork safety". `fork_prepare` ends holding one `lock::LockHeld` token per lock of the fork lock list, and `fork_release` (the parent and child handlers) hands every token back through `RawLock::unlock_held`, ending with none; the child rewrites the background state only under its token and keeps its lock invariant. Premises: the futex mutex contract and the atfork handoff (`api::axiom_fork_resumed_state`, `axiom_fork_resumed`); the frozen arena table under the creation lock (`api::arena_held`, `count_held`) is proved since package P8, from the lock cell's permission the hold keeps. Not covered: prepare-handler termination, and compatibility with other `pthread_atfork` handlers (the handoff premise assumes those registered before challoc's take no allocator lock). The nesting order is proved by theorem 33, "Lock order is acyclic". Still open: the list's completeness (the test `fork_lock_list_matches_the_source_tree` checks it).
- **Arena isolation** (done, theorem 14): A slab slot belongs to the arena its group was handed to (`class::slab_owner`, fixed by `take_group`'s owner-word store and read back by every owner load, two trusted wrappers). `ClassState::plan` reserves only in slabs the arena owns (the class lock invariant now holds `owns(s) ==> slab_owner == arena`); `release_owned` releases only the arena's slots and `release_with`, `Shared::free_direct` abort (`wrong_arena`) otherwise; `release_routed` follows the owners and its termination is now proved. Not claimed: that `MALLOCX_ARENA(a)` returns a slot of arena `a`; sized frees put foreign slots in the home cache (design 6.3), so a result-owner promise would need cache flags and contents in its statement. Still open: carry the owner from the picks through `finish` to `claim`'s and `malloc`'s results, and tie a huge run's descriptor arena to `alloc_run`'s argument (live-block run token).
- **Re-entrant heap registration** (done, theorem 15): The TLS slot is a ghost `sys::tls::TlsSlot` token. `ThreadKey::set` requires a sentinel in the slot, and at a sentinel `heap_for_thread_in` returns `None` and leaves the token unchanged: no slot write and no `Heap::create` call. `existing_heap_in` takes the token shared and returns `None` at a sentinel. The `pthread_setspecific` wrapper models nested entries as these cores holding the lent token. Still open: `Heap::create` making no call that can allocate is checked by inspection, not by Verus; the theorem does not state that no allocator lock is held across `ThreadKey::set`.
- **No allocator lock across external calls** (done, theorem 16): Partly premised: Verus checks possession of the `Unlocked` permit, not that no lock is held. Callbacks, `malloc_message`, and `fwrite` reached from `malloc_stats_print` or `malloc_info` run while the calling thread holds no allocator lock. These calls can allocate, so holding a lock could deadlock. Every report call out of the allocator is in `api::print::emit`, which requires the ghost permit `Unlocked`; only the three entry points mint one (`axiom_unlocked`), and the report's `Sink` holds it. That the permit's holder holds no lock rests on premises that a source test checks (the report code takes no lock, guards stay in their scope, only the fork handlers take a bare `RawLock`). Two tests re-enter the allocator from every call out, so a lock a report takes, held across a call, fails them. Given those premises, no path calls out with a lock held. Still open: a ledger of held locks that Verus checks needs a `Tracked` token through `Lock::lock` and every path to it. The lock-order candidate below needs the same ledger, so the two could share it. On main stronger: `Unlocked` and `axiom_unlocked` are gone; `api::print::emit` requires a verified `LockOrder` token with `ord.top() == 0` (no lock held), so the source test of the premises is gone with them. The two re-entry tests are not on main (wolfi-dev/challoc#397).
- **Exact stats** (done, theorem 22): `collect_into`, `collect_all` and `mallinfo2` hand out (ghost `rd`) the figures they read and report their true sums (`stats::arena_exact`, `summed`, `totals_exact`); the ghost ledger `stats::ledger::Books` steps through every counter update and slot move, keeps its invariant in any interleaving, and at a quiescent state makes `Σ_arenas (claimed - released)` the cached plus live slots and each `large` word its live runs; `theorem_exact_stats` sums the `Live` tokens (counted once each by `heap::live::lemma_all_apart`) to the reported `allocated`. The huge lock's `stats.runs`/`run_pages` equal the run authorities' sizes in every lock value (`huge::theorem_huge_stats`). `mallinfo2`'s doc table now says `hblkhd` includes the slab classes above `BIN_MAX` (so it can be non-zero with `hblks` zero). Still open: a code-level ledger (the counters updated inside the bitmap words' invariants) instead of the model and its quiescent-read premise, and the no-clamp argument for `bin_stats`.
- **Bad input** (done, the B column): returning calls satisfy the pointer-shape checks and the selected argument checks, under the live-pointer contract (`axiom_c_claim`); "abort" below means proved not to return normally (reaching `SIGABRT` rather than hanging is not proved). Not checked by the code either: an ABA free (undetectable). Proved: pointers that are not block starts (foreign, misaligned by `theorem_free_target_aligned`, interior by `Live::theorem_interior`) abort; a slot freed with another class's size or a bad alignment aborts (`hint_ok` on `free_sized`, `free_aligned_sized`, `sdallocx` and the sized `delete`s, from the completeness of `Regions::slab_slot` and `classify`; for a live slot, as a non-live one rests on `axiom_c_claim`); a live huge run freed with a size that does not round up to its pages aborts (`huge_hint_ok` of C's token on the sized frees' cores, from `check_size_hint`'s `Huge::lookup` with the run token and `classify`'s exactness on huge page starts, #127); `MALLOCX_TCACHE` ids of `MAX_TCACHES` or more abort (`tcache_checked`, `bad_tcache`); a non-power-of-two `align_val_t`, 0 included, gets no block (and, by inspection of `new_aligned_block`, no handler call). Excluded by the C contract (`axiom_c_claim`), not proved: stale and double frees of slot or huge page starts, and interior page starts of huge runs; the run-time checks stay. Still open: arena and cache existence for `MALLOCX_ARENA` and `MALLOCX_TCACHE` ids below the limit; and the queries' stale-slot and interior-page aborts.
- **Huge free extents are maximally coalesced** (done, theorem 17): At the huge lock invariant; nothing is exported per call. `huge::Inner::wf` now states that no two free descriptors touch (`coalesced`) and that every page of a free extent is in the free-page bitmap (`free_marked`); `Inner::theorem_coalesced` states it ([THEOREMS.md](THEOREMS.md) section 17, added by wolfi-dev/challoc#422). `find` proves it returns fresh pages only when no free extent touches the mark, `free_before`/`free_after` find every free neighbour, and `take`, `free_run` and `purge_extent` keep it. No counterexample: alignment holes at the mark cannot touch a free extent. Ghost-only. The converse of `free_marked` (a free bit belongs to a free extent) is now proved too ("Huge free bitmap is exact", below).
- **Pressure never lengthens decay** (done, theorem 18): At a fixed limit, for the pure functions only: for a fixed effective cgroup limit and configured period, higher usage never gives a lower band (`lemma_band_monotone`) nor a longer effective decay period (`policy::theorem_pressure_never_lengthens_decay`), over exact specs of `usage_ppm`, `band` and `effective_decay_ns`. Not about the stored policy the purge reads or timely purging. Open: changing limits, the `Policy` atomics round trip (`Policy::apply` then `Policy::band`, not exact at the 70% edge, below) under concurrent evaluation, and `band`'s reading of a 0 limit as no limit where `thp::forced_by_limit` reads it as a small one.
- **Decay deadline soundness** (done, theorem 30): Per gate, over the values its loads see. `Decayer::decay` ensures its result is `Decayer::spec_decay` (closed) of its three loads, and `theorem_decay_deadline` proves `None` exactly when the oldest-`Empty` load is 0 or the walk ran and the load after it reads 0, and a deadline `t` with `now <= t <= max(oldest, now) + step`, so at most one step of the current period past `now` when the oldest member is stamped by then: a `next_walk` more than one step away (set under a period since shortened by `set_decay_ms` or a pressure band) is never returned. `theorem_decay_deadline_under_pressure` composes it with theorem 18 (higher usage, no longer step), and `Policy::effective_decay_ns` ensures `ns <= base`. `ClassState::decay_with` and `Huge::decay_with` carry the contract, but its witnesses are existential, so callers get only `t >= now`. No counterexample. Open: the stamps and coarse clock as ghost state, so a caller can discharge `oldest <= now`; the `min` over gates in `Arena::decay_with` and `purge::decay`, hence the background thread's wait, by contract (`map_or` closures, `earliest`); a ghost walk history for "at most one walk per step"; the futex wake-up (liveness, "Purge within the decay time" below). Seen while reading: `mallctl arena.0.decay` passes the huge allocator `shared.decay_ns()`, not `huge_decay_ns()`, so it ignores `huge_dirty_decay_ms`, unlike `purge::decay`.
- **Purge never discards live data** (done, theorem 19): every `purge::Batch` carries a ghost predicate `free`, fixed by its maker, and `discard` hands `madvise` only bytes it admits. Huge batches admit pages outside the run-page authority, so `RunToken::theorem_purge_apart` and `Live::theorem_purge_huge` prove no live block's byte is discarded. Class batches admit slabs the arena's class lock records as idle (`Fresh`, `Empty`, `Purged`). Still open: tie idleness to the slot tokens, which needs the descriptor's lock-free `live` counter as ghost-tracked state (credits minted by `take`, deposited by the claim's `fetch_or`, released by `fetch_and` and `sub_live`) through the claim and release paths; and theorem 6's purge premise (eager discards minting `Zeroed` tokens held per `Purged` slab or extent, and a ghost link from `LAZY_EVER` to the lazy batches).
- **`MALLOCX_TCACHE_NONE` bypasses thread caches** (done, theorem 20): `Heap::caches_same` (same cache permissions, headers, stacks, entries and slot tokens; `lemma_caches_same` spells it out per header and entry) is ensured by the counters the direct paths call on the thread's heap (`count_direct_alloc`, `count_direct_free`, `count_uncached_*`) and by `alloc_huge_in`, `free_huge`; the direct paths (`mallocx::alloc`/`free` on `Tcache::None`, `free_direct`, `Shared::alloc_direct`/`free_direct`) reach no other heap and no explicit cache, and move the slot's token between the class's bitmap words and the caller's `Live`. Machine code is unchanged; `tcache_none_leaves_every_cache_alone` (`api/jemalloc.rs`, added by wolfi-dev/challoc#418) checks it at run time. Still open: Verus does not link two `existing_heap` references to one heap, so "reaches no other heap" is by inspection, and the class-state source and sink are in the bodies of `Shared::alloc_direct`/`free_direct`, not their `ensures`. Valkey's defragmenter relies on this behavior.
- **MIB round-trip** (done, theorem 21): One recursive spec, `api::mallctl::resolve`, names the node a MIB reaches; `walk` is the same descent matched against a name's dotted components (`split`, proved unique). `name_to_mib` ensures its MIB walks the name to the node it returns and resolves to it; `mib_to_node` succeeds exactly on MIBs that resolve and returns their node; `ctl` requires its node to be its MIB's, discharged at `mallctl` and `mallctlbymib`. `theorem_mib_round_trip` adds that every entry is the one its component names (`entry_named`): at a numeric level (arena and bin indices) its decimal value. Premise: `root` returns the same nodes every call. Documented, not changed: `mallctlnametomib` returns `EINVAL` for a short buffer, whereas jemalloc 5.3.0 returns 0 with a partial MIB. Still open: `name_to_mib`'s completeness (that it succeeds on every name the tree spells), and `ensures` on the C entry points' return codes.
- **Cache routing** (done, theorem 26): A ghost `Heap::keys` gives each `others` node its key, and `Heap::list_ok` now states the keys pairwise distinct and never the home arena (it said only that the node pointers are distinct). `Heap::pop`, `pop_compact` and `pop_compact_run` require `Heap::poppable` (the home header, or a header of a node whose key is not `FOREIGN`); `other_caches` ensures its node is keyed by the requested arena (or that none is), `cache_slot` and `ensure_cache` that their header `routes` the requested arena, and `theorem_cache_routing` that a header routes at most one arena and that no poppable header is the `FOREIGN` node's. `alloc_in`/`alloc_other` require `arena != FOREIGN`, discharged from `Flags::decode` (an explicit arena is below 4095). `set_arena`'s exchange is proved to keep the keys distinct and off the home arena. `node_arena` returns the ghost key, read through the permission to the node's `arena` field (`Heap::heads`). Ghost only; machine code unchanged. Not claimed: arena purity of `malloc`'s results, since sized frees push other arenas' slots into the home cache (design 6.3). Still open: link the requested arena to `pop` itself (a ghost argument), cover `pop_fast` by contract rather than by its body, and make "the `FOREIGN` cache is released whole" (two proof assertions) an `ensures`.
- **Lowest-address huge placement** (done, theorem 23): `find`'s contract under the huge lock; no entry point exports it. `Huge::find` ensures its start fits (aligned, inside the region, every page in a free extent or at or past the mark) and no lower page does, and `None` only when no page fits; `align_page` ensures it returns the first aligned page. Coalescing does the free-extent cases (`Inner::lemma_fit_extent`). No counterexample: the early return at an extent touching the mark is correct even when the alignment carries the start past it; tests `alignment_past_a_trailing_extent_takes_fresh_pages` and `placement_is_the_lowest_fit` (2,000 random steps against a brute-force oracle, NULLs and purges included). Ghost-only. Open: export to `alloc_aligned` (NULL after `find` on metadata or commit failure is not excluded).
- **Huge free bitmap is exact** (done, theorem 29): At the huge lock invariant. `Inner::wf` gains `free_exact`, the converse of `free_marked`: every set bit of the free-page bitmap is a page of a free (`Empty` or `Purged`) extent, so none is on an `Active` page, between extents, or at or past `next_page` (`Inner::theorem_free_exact`). `took_free` and `freed_free` became equivalences (the set after a take or a free, exactly), `Take::touch_ok` states which pieces cover what, `lemma_took_coalesced` and `lemma_freed_coalesced` prove the new conjunct, `purge_extent` keeps it, and `class::Set::new` ensures an empty view. With it `Inner::extent_at` requires the bit set and `ends_by` its page, and its "free page has no extent" abort is proved unreachable (`assert(false)` in the branch), closing theorem 23's abort gap. No counterexample. Ghost-only. Open: export it per call (no entry point's `ensures` mentions it).
- **Huge retain cap** (done, theorem 31): at the huge lock's release inside `Huge::free_run`, `retain_cap` holds of the lock values before and after, against the `retain_max` the free read: a run with more usable bytes than the cap, or freed under `purge_on_free` (`huge_dirty_decay_ms:0`), takes the branch that pushes its pages into a fresh purge batch and flushes it, and its pages lie in a `Purged` extent with `dirty_at == 0` or, merged with an `Empty` neighbour, in an `Empty` extent with that neighbour's age (the `purge_on_free` half rests on `pof`, defined from the branch taken, being the flag read: by the condition's code, not proved); a run at or below the cap is not discarded and lies in an `Empty` extent aged `now`, or an `Empty` neighbour's age. The `docs/tuning.md` large-buffer guarantee. Not "ends `Purged`" outright: a discarded run merged with an `Empty` neighbour stays `Empty` (counted dirty, discarded again by decay), by design; tests `retain_cap_decides_purged_or_empty_at_free` and `a_freed_run_takes_an_empty_neighbours_state_and_age`. Exec: `retain_max()` bound to a local before the condition (same loads, same short circuit; library identical). Still open: that the branch hands the run's bytes to `madvise` is read from `Batch::push`/`flush`'s bodies; a ghost record (`Batch::holds_range` from `push`, a `sent` set by `flush`) verified in `purge` and `huge` but broke `class::decay`'s `Purger::visit` (`purge_group`'s page-group precondition) under `quantum8`, so harden that proof first. No `ensures` exports the theorem (a ghost result through `free_huge`); the cap and flag are the values read, tied to the configured ones by inspection (`set_retain_max`/`set_purge_on_free` only in `Shared::init` before the `Huge` is published), since vstd leaves std atomics' values unconstrained; `pof` is defined from the branch taken. On main: since wolfi-dev/challoc#416, `retain_cap`, `empty_nbr_at` and `lemma_retain_cap` are in `src/huge/mod.rs`, and `free_run` proves `retain_cap` at its lock release; both tests are in `src/huge/tests.rs`. Main differs in two ways. The run is discarded by `purge::discard_one`, a purge batch of one, whose result has no contract, so the first conjunct only bounds a discard (`discarded ==> usable > cap || pof`) and does not say that a run above the cap is discarded; a run whose discard fails stays `Empty`. And `pof` is `free_run`'s binding of what the `purge_on_free` read returned, read only at or below the cap and false when unread, not a value defined from the branch taken. Follow-up: the "Theorem 31 (huge retain cap)" item of wolfi-dev/challoc#397.
- **Lock order is acyclic** (done, theorem 33): every lock has a ghost rank (`lock::rank_*`, a `RawLock` ghost field fixed at creation and tied to its owner by type invariants), and every blocking acquisition requires a `lock::LockOrder` token whose bound is below the lock's rank. `Lock::lock` freezes the caller's token for the guard's lifetime and hands out one at the lock's rank; `RawLock::lock_held` raises a root token. The token is threaded through the slow allocation and free paths, huge, arena and heap creation, explicit caches, purge, statistics, `mallctl` and `fork_prepare`. No counterexample. Ghost only; machine code unchanged. Needs an exec change: today `ThreadKey::set` registers nothing and the heap is still published.

- **Fork handlers restore the signal mask** (done, theorem 39): `fork_prepare` blocks every blockable signal before its first lock and saves the mask in force at its entry under every allocator lock; the release reads it under every lock, keeps every blockable signal blocked through its last unlock, and leaves exactly the slot's mask, in parent and child. A ghost `sys::SigMask` token (field private to `sys::sigmask`) is the calling thread's mask, changed only by `block_all_signals`/`set_signal_mask`, whose new `ensures` rest on two trusted raw-call wrappers (`sigprocmask_block`, `sigprocmask_setmask`: the kernel's `rt_sigprocmask`). `FORK_SIGMASK` stays a relaxed `AtomicU64` with verified accessors: the store's value is recorded in the fork hold (`ForkHeld::saved`), and the release's hold carries the slot's `vspec::word::WordPerm` (`ForkHeld::has_slot`), through which `saved_sigmask` loads it; `save_sigmask` stores through the same `fork_slot` and requires a hold without the permission (`no_slot`) (`word::load`, `r == perm.value()`); both accessors require `fork_holds`, so two threads forking with different masks each get their own back (test `concurrent_fork_handlers_restore_each_threads_mask`, not on main). Ghost only; machine code unchanged. Still open: one-shot use of the minting axioms (as "Fork-handler axioms are one-shot" below), and the background thread's spawn (trusted body).
- **Fork-handler axioms are one-shot:** A second call of `axiom_fork_resumed_state` or `api::resume::resumed` (the one caller of `axiom_fork_resumed`, private to that module, which it passes `fork_slot()`) would mint a second token for a held lock (and `resumed` a second permission to `FORK_SIGMASK`), and a second call of `axiom_fork_resumed_sig` or `axiom_thread_mask` a second `SigMask` token, and still verify. Consume a permit minted once so the handoffs of theorems 13 and 36 cannot be double-counted.
